## Context

What situation forced a decision? What constraints were real at the time — team size, budget,
deadline, existing commitments? Someone reading this in a year has none of that context, and
without it the decision looks arbitrary.

## Decision

What we're doing. One paragraph, plainly. No hedging.

### Option B — <name> (rejected)

What it is. **Why it lost** — be specific. "More complex" is not a reason; "requires a second
service and a queue we'd have to operate" is.

## Consequences

What this makes easy. What it makes hard. What we're now committed to that we weren't before.

Include the unpleasant ones. A decision doc that only lists upsides is marketing.

## What would change our mind

The concrete trigger that should cause someone to revisit this. Scale threshold, a cost
crossing a line, a dependency being abandoned, a constraint disappearing.

Without this, the decision becomes dogma, and dogma is how a good call at 10 users becomes a
bad one at 10,000.
